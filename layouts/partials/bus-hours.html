{{- /* bus-hours — when in the day a route's journeys get cut short. Renders `curtailment_detail.per_10_days_by_hour`: 24 values, each the number of curtailments in that hour per 10 days the route was observed. DRAWN PER DAY, NOT PER TEN DAYS, and that is the whole point of this comment. The contract's unit is an analyst's normalisation, and putting it on the page beside a four-week total meant the reader was holding two different units and asked to reconcile them. Route 38 read "345 in four weeks" next to "peak 15.0 per 10 days" — and 345 over 28 days is 123 per 10 days, so the two looked irreconcilable. They were not: 15.0 was the peak in a single HOUR, and the label never said so. Every hour divided by ten gives journeys per hour on an average day, and those bars now SUM TO the daily rate printed above the chart. A reader can see twelve a day and watch the bars add up to twelve. Server-rendered, like `bus-spark.html` and for the same reason: these numbers are the point of the block, and a reader with no JavaScript should get them. The figures are also listed underneath in the page, so this is the picture and not the record. An hour with `null` was not observed enough to say — shaded, never drawn as a zero. An hour with 0.0 IS a measurement and draws as a flat baseline, which is why every test below is against nil and not for truthiness: in Go templates `if 0.0` is false, and "no curtailments in this hour" is the commonest and most welcome reading on the whole page. Params: values (24 slice, nulls allowed), label */ -}} {{- $raw := .values -}} {{- $n := len $raw -}} {{- /* Per 10 days in the contract; per day on the page. */ -}} {{- $v := slice -}} {{- range $raw }}{{ $v = $v | append (cond (eq . nil) nil (div . 10.0)) }}{{ end -}} {{- $hi := 0.0 }}{{ $peakHour := 0 -}} {{- range $i, $y := $v }}{{ if and (ne $y nil) (gt $y $hi) }}{{ $hi = $y }}{{ $peakHour = $i }}{{ end }}{{ end -}} {{- /* A y scale, because without one the bars had shape but no size: the note said "number cut short in that hour on an average day" and nothing on the chart said what any bar came to. The smallest round step from a fixed list that clears the busiest hour in at most four ticks — a fixed three ticks put a busiest hour of 1.5 under an axis running to 3. */ -}} {{- $step := 0.0 }}{{ $nt := 1 -}} {{- range slice 0.1 0.2 0.5 1.0 2.0 5.0 10.0 20.0 50.0 100.0 -}} {{- $k := int (math.Ceil (div $hi .)) -}} {{- if and (eq $step 0.0) (le $k 4) }}{{ $step = . }}{{ $nt = math.Max 1 $k }}{{ end -}} {{- end -}} {{- if eq $step 0.0 }}{{ $step = 100.0 }}{{ $nt = 4 }}{{ end -}} {{- $nt = int $nt -}} {{- $top := mul $step (float $nt) -}} {{- $dec := cond (lt $step 1.0) 1 0 -}} {{- $W := 480.0 }}{{ $H := 132.0 }}{{ $plot := 96.0 }}{{ $ptop := 18.0 -}} {{- $L := 30.0 }}{{ $pad := 4.0 -}} {{- $bstep := div (sub (sub $W $L) $pad) (float $n) -}} {{- $bw := mul $bstep 0.66 -}}
