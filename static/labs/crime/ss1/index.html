About this data

About this data. Search locations come from police.uk, which moves every record to a fixed nearby anonymised point, so positions are street-level approximations — read the hexes, not the dots. About 8.2% of Met searches in 2021–2025 carry no location at all and cannot appear on the map; almost all of that gap is before October 2024 (roughly 10.6% of records unmapped in 2021–2023, about 0.7% in 2025), so earlier years look slightly thinner than they were, and the Met published no data for November 2022. A search is an enforcement decision, not a measure of how much crime happens in a place: comparing the two maps shows where police attention goes, which is exactly the question — not a crime rate.

Reading the heights. Every measure is scaled to its own maximum, so a full-height search tower and a full-height crime tower are not the same count — compare the geography, not the heights across measures.

Tooltip companion. While a search measure is up, the tooltip also shows the same hex's thefts from the person — the sharpest street-level crime signal — and all searches per offence; while a crime measure is up it shows the hex's total searches. “Positive outcome” means any recorded outcome other than no-further-action.

Sources. police.uk stop and search and street-level crime, Metropolitan Police and City of London Police, 2021–2025.
