Geography-morph workbench

This is a temporary prototype workbench for morphing between geography levels on the census map.

  • v0/ — minimal harness: split/merge value-morph on the OA basis (buttons, duration/easing controls, HUD)
  • v1/ — full census map with the curtain replaced by the morph (?morph=0 restores the curtain; ?hud=1 overlay)
  • v2/ — boundary-warp experiment: wards crack apart out of a borough block and heal back. Two verdicts. deck.gl's own transitions cannot tween getPolygon here — declaring one blanks the layer outright, silently (?postrans=1 reproduces it). Driving the warp from requestAnimationFrame instead does animate it, at ~25–46 fps. Outlines and values cannot move at the same time, so each choreography plays them as separate beats. Stills of the crack: ?s0=0.92, ?s0=0.8. Numbers in tools/morph-lab/captures/v2/RESULTS.txt.

Driving guide

The three prototypes are built and self-verified. What's left is aesthetic — judgment calls only a human eye can make. Drive each page from the links below, then work the checklist underneath.

v0 — minimal harness

Buttons, duration, and easing controls over a bare split/merge value-morph on the OA basis.

Morph proven to slide, not snap: mid-flight progression 33.8% → 73.3% → 98.0%, endpoints land ≤1 px off, plateau (OA painted with borough values vs. the true borough map) MAD 0.045/255.

Zooming mid-morph in v0 (only) restarts the transition — v0 predates the polyData fix that v1 carries. Judge that behaviour in v1, not v0.

v2 — boundary warp, ward↔borough

Wards crack apart out of a borough block and heal back, instead of a value-only morph.

deck.gl's built-in position transitions BLANK the layer outright (repro preserved at ?postrans=1). The CPU-driven warp works instead: ~25–46 fps, but outlines and values can't move together, so the choreography plays as three separate beats (crack → values → heal).
